1810 – 1851 · THE EARLY YEARS

Early History

George Coles — The Man Who Changed Prince Edward Island 1. Who was George Coles? George Coles (September 20, 1810 – August 21, 1875) was a Canadian politician, businessman, and one of the most influential political figures in Prince Edward Island's history. He became the first Premier of Prince Edward Island under responsible government in 1851. Coles was particularly important for: - Fighting for responsible government. - Establishing publicly funded education. - Supporting political reform. - Challenging the power of wealthy landowners. - Representing Prince Edward Island at the Confederation conferences. He is remembered as one of the Fathers of Confederation. 2. Early Life (1810–1842) George Coles was born on September 20, 1810, in Charlottetown Royalty, Prince Edward Island. His parents were James Coles and Sarah Tally. He grew up on his father's farm and received relatively little formal education. At 19 years old, he travelled to England. While there, he married Mercy Haine on August 14, 1833. After returning to Prince Edward Island, Coles began developing his business career. He eventually became a successful: - Merchant. - Farmer. - Brewer. - Distiller. - Business owner. His experience as a businessman helped shape his political views, particularly his interest in improving opportunities for ordinary Islanders.

3. Political Career George Coles entered politics in 1842 when he was elected to the Prince Edward Island House of Assembly. He became a leading member of the Island's Liberal movement and worked alongside other reformers, including Edward Whelan. His political career focused on changing how Prince Edward Island was governed. One of his biggest goals was to give elected representatives more control over the government rather than allowing the British-appointed governor to dominate political decisions. He served as Premier during three periods: Period Position 1851–1854 Premier 1855–1859 Premier 1867–1868 Premier 4. Responsible Government (1851) This was arguably George Coles' most important political achievement. Before responsible government, the British-appointed governor held significant executive power. Responsible government changed this system. Under the new system: - The government needed the support of elected representatives. - The Executive Council became accountable to the elected assembly. - The Premier and government could no longer govern without maintaining the confidence of the legislature. In 1851, Prince Edward Island finally achieved responsible government after years of political disagreement. George Coles became its first Premier under this new system. This was an important step toward democratic self-government in Canada. 5. Free Education Act (1852) George Coles believed that education should be accessible to children regardless of their family's financial situation. In 1852, his government introduced the Free Education Act. Before this legislation, families and local communities had to contribute toward teachers' salaries, and students could face tuition fees. The new legislation: - Removed tuition fees. - Made the government responsible for teachers' salaries. - Helped make education accessible to poorer families. - Supported the development of public education. The results were significant: school enrollment doubled within two years. This made the Free Education Act one of the most important achievements of Coles' government. 6. The Land Question During George Coles' lifetime, a major problem affected Prince Edward Island: land ownership. Large areas of the Island were controlled by wealthy landowners, many of whom lived outside the Island. Many ordinary Islanders rented the land they farmed instead of owning it. This created several problems: - Farmers had to pay rent to landowners. - Many families struggled to obtain ownership of farmland. - Wealthy landowners held considerable economic and political influence. Coles wanted to reduce the power of the landlord system. In 1853, his government passed the Land Purchase Act. It allowed the government to purchase large estates and resell portions of the land to tenants and settlers. However, his efforts did not completely solve the land problem. 7. George Coles and Canadian Confederation In September 1864, George Coles participated in the Charlottetown Conference, where political leaders discussed the possibility of uniting British North American colonies. He also attended the Quebec Conference later that year. However, there is an important detail about his position: George Coles did not simply support Confederation. He wanted any proposed union to address Prince Edward Island's land ownership problems. When the proposed Confederation agreement failed to provide the land reforms he wanted, Coles opposed the Quebec resolutions. Prince Edward Island did not join Confederation in 1867. It eventually became part of Canada in 1873. Coles is nevertheless recognized as a Father of Confederation because of his participation in the conferences.

8. Later Life and Death George Coles' political career began declining around 1868 because of deteriorating health. He eventually retired from political leadership. He died on August 21, 1875, in Charlottetown Royalty, Prince Edward Island, at the age of 64. Although he was no longer politically active, his contributions to education, democracy, and political reform continued to influence Prince Edward Island. 1851 – 1875 · LASTING IMPACT

His Legacy

9. His Legacy George Coles helped shape the political and educational foundations of Prince Edward Island. His legacy includes: 1. Democracy: Helped establish responsible government. 2. Education: Made publicly funded education more accessible. 3. Political reform: Challenged existing political and economic structures. 4. Land reform: Attempted to improve land ownership opportunities. 5. Confederation: Participated in the historic discussions that led to Canadian Confederation. He remains an important figure in Prince Edward Island's history. As the first Premier of Prince Edward Island and a Father of Confederation, Coles helped shape free public education, land reform, and modern democratic governance.
